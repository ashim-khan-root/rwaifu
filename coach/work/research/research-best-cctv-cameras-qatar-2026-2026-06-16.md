## 2. Regulatory Environment and Standards  

### 2.1 National Cybersecurity Strategy  

Qatar’s **National Cybersecurity Strategy (2023‑2026)** mandates that all surveillance systems for critical infrastructure must employ **encrypted video streams (AES‑256)**, support **secure remote access**, and retain footage locally for a minimum of **30 days** before optional cloud archiving [2]. The strategy also requires **certified installers** to hold a Ministry of Interior (MoI) license, a stipulation that has driven the professionalisation of the installation market [9].

### 2‑2. GCC Technical Guidelines  

The GCC’s **Unified Security Standards (2022)** prescribe the use of **IP‑based cameras** for any new public‑sector project, with a minimum resolution of **1080p** and **PoE** power delivery to reduce cabling complexity [2]. These guidelines have been adopted by Qatar’s Public Works Authority (Ashghal) for the Doha Metro and by the Qatar Civil Aviation Authority for airport surveillance.

### 2‑3. Data‑Localisation & Privacy  

Qatar’s **Data Protection Law (2024)** requires that video footage containing personally identifiable information (PII) be stored on servers physically located within the country, unless a specific exemption is granted. This has spurred the growth of **local data‑center services** and limited the use of overseas cloud video‑storage platforms for high‑sensitivity applications [2].

### 2‑4. Licensing & Certification  

- **Installation License** – Issued by the MoI after a 40‑hour technical course covering wiring standards, PoE, and cybersecurity basics.  
- **Equipment Certification** – Devices must be listed on the **Qatar Communications Regulatory Authority (CRA) Approved Equipment Register**. Dahua’s DH‑IPC‑PDW3849‑A180‑AS‑PV, for example, received CRA approval in early 2024 [4].
